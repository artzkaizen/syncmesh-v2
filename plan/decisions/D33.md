## Context

A `join` was `[8, versions, peerId, cursors, interest]` and the room believed it
(`packages/relay/src/frames.ts`, `connection.ts:onJoin`). Nothing tied the bytes to the key: the
`peerId` *is* an Ed25519 public key, and the join carried no signature under it. Three things
followed. Anyone could take a device's seat and be paged what was meant for it. Because a newer
join supersedes the older socket — `room.clients.get(peer)?.socket.close("superseded by a newer
join")` — anyone could hang up on the real device from anywhere on the internet, forever, with one
frame per reconnect. And the relay link skipped the handshake every peer-to-peer link runs
(`transport/src/handshake.ts`), so it relied on `wss://` for confidentiality and on nothing for
identity. The posture's `verifyJoin(request, room)` sees the HTTP upgrade, not the key, so a
bearer ticket there says who may open a socket and nothing about who is on it.

Found by the 2026-09-26 code audit (`docs/research/sync-engines.md` §5 in paper-canvas), gap №2.

## Options

- **A · Sign a timestamped join, no round trip.** The join signs `(room, peerId, cursors, at)`;
  the room checks the signature and a freshness window. Rejected: replayable within the window
  across sockets, and the window is a clock — the thing D34 is about not trusting.
- **B · The room challenges first; the join signs the challenge and its own body.** A new control
  frame `challenge` (tag 19, 32 random bytes) is the room's first frame on every socket. The join
  gains a sixth element: the named key's signature over `"syncmesh/relay/join/v2" ‖ nonce ‖
  core`, where `core` is the join's own four elements canonically encoded. Fresh per socket, so a
  captured proof opens nothing later; over the body, so a forwarder cannot re-cursor it; domain
  separated, so it cannot be lifted from a link hello or an event, nor lifted out to stand in for
  one.
- **C · Run the link handshake over the relay socket.** Signed X25519 + XChaCha20, as every radio
  link does; identity falls out of it and the link gets confidentiality the relay no longer
  borrows from TLS. The right end state, and larger than a fix: the room forwards *bytes* it did
  not seal to other clients, a hibernating Durable Object would have to persist session keys
  across sleeps, and every frame would cost a seal. Deferred, not rejected — see below.

## Decision

**B**, as protocol version 2. `RELAY_PROTOCOL_VERSIONS` becomes `[2]`; a client offers 2 and
sends no join until a challenge has arrived (`transport.ts:join`). A room admits a bare join only
where its operator lists 1 in `versions` — and even there a proof that fails is a refusal, because
a wrong signature is never the absence of one. The refusal is typed (`unproven`), fatal to the
socket, and reached *before* the seat is taken: a name nobody proved cannot close the socket of
the device that owns it.

Hosts: the Bun host challenges on `open`. The Durable Object host binds a socket at `join` rather
than on its first frame, so the challenge is the first thing the socket sees, and keeps the nonce
in the resume attachment (`[[nonce], join, …grants]`) so a socket that sleeps between challenge
and join, or whose join is replayed after an eviction, still verifies.

Frozen: the `challenge` frame and a signed join are in `frames.test.ts`'s vector; the proof's
bytes are in `conformance/` beside the receipt and grant vectors (README rule 3).

## What this costs

- One round trip before the first join on a socket — the challenge — on a link that already pays
  a TCP and a TLS handshake.
- A version bump. There are no deployed v1 relays outside this repository; a room that must serve
  one lists `versions: [1, 2]` and is, in the room's own words, taking a device's name on trust.
- The resume attachment grows by 34 bytes.

## Watch out

- **This is identity, not confidentiality.** The relay still reads every frame it forwards, and
  the link's secrecy is still `wss://`'s. C landed as **D36** (protocol v3): the challenge is
  redundant with the hello's signature there and is sent only by rooms that still list 2.
- **A proof binds a join to a socket, not a person to a room.** Who may be in the room at all is
  still the posture (`verifyJoin`) and, once E24's confidentiality decision is written, the grant.
- **The fake sockets in tests had to learn to buffer.** A real socket queues what the server sends
  before the client subscribes; `dialTo` and the Durable Object fake did not, and the challenge
  arrived before anyone was listening. Any new fake must do the same, or its transport never joins.
