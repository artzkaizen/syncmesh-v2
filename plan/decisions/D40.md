## Context

E24 has one item left open in so many words: _"Write the confidentiality posture into RFC-0017:
`verifyJoin` + per-partition encryption, or 'a room name is a bearer secret', in those words."_
Its Watch-out says the same thing harder: _"Any socket can currently join any room and pull the
whole log. Decide the posture; silence is not one."_

Re-verified on `main` at `638eed6` (2026-11-07):

- **Identity is proven, membership is not.** D33/D36 make a join prove the key it names, and a v3
  socket is a sealed link. Nothing after that asks whether the proven key may be in the room.
  `createRoomAccess` answers `admitsJoin` with `(await posture.verifyJoin?.(…)) ?? true`
  (`packages/relay/src/posture.ts:56`) — an absent hook admits everyone — and every host
  (`startRelay`, `adapters/relay-node`, `adapters/cloudflare-do`) passes the posture through with
  `announce` defaulting to `true`.
- **The relay holds no issuer key and verifies no grant.** `GrantCache` reads `readGrantOrigin`
  without a signature check, only to decide which bytes to forward.
- **The relay already narrows by partition.** A join carries an `Interest` (partitions only,
  `engine/src/interest.ts`); catch-up filters by it and hands back a D23 scoped coverage
  (`relay/src/catchup.ts`), and live fan-out obeys the same `wants` (`relay/src/connection.ts`).
  The partition is in the clear in every event core — sealing covers the payload, not the
  partition — so the relay can narrow by partition without reading a payload. What it narrows by
  today is what the device _asked_ for, never what it is _allowed_.
- **Peer to peer sends everything.** `bridgeFramedLink` offers every outbound event to every link
  and answers cursors with the whole diff; the far side's grant is in the registry and is never
  consulted on the send side. Receivers check the _author's_ grant (`PartitionNotGranted`), which
  is write admission, not read.
- **Sealed partitions exist end to end.** `partition(…, { sealed: true })`, content keys wrapped to
  the device inside the grant (grant key 8), a `KeyRing` with epochs, `PartitionSealed` on a
  device without the key, and frozen `sealing-vectors.json`. What does not exist: any API that
  mints a partition key, wraps it into an issued grant (`mesh.grants.issue` has no `keys`), or
  rotates an epoch when a member is removed.
- **RFC-0024 §4.6 already designed the join half** ("grant-gated rooms": public `trust` config on
  the room, the grant is the ticket, `ungranted` refusal, deny list). It is `proposed`, not decided,
  and its per-event write half (R1–R4) is write admission, outside this decision.
- **E06's `fields` and the ungranted-mode `deny`** are both unbuilt / untested.

The consumer is Company OS: one tenant per company workspace, and inside it teams, projects,
customers, private DMs and HR records — so _who reads what_ is per partition, changes daily
(people join and leave projects), and for DMs and HR must hold against the relay operator too.

## The question, in four parts

1. **Where read permission is enforced** — the posture.
2. **How a room maps to partitions, and what the ticket is.**
3. **What happens on revocation** — sockets, peers, keys.
4. **`fields`** — what a field-level read rule can honestly mean on this mesh.

## 1 · The posture

- **A · "A room name is a bearer secret."** Write it into RFC-0017 in those words and change no
  code. Whoever knows the name reads the room; confidentiality is the app's job (unguessable room
  names, one room per partition). Honest, free, and wrong for Company OS: a member removed from a
  project still knows the name, and nothing stops them re-joining.
- **B · Grant-gated rooms.** The relay is given public trust config — the issuer keys whose grants
  it honours, never a private key. After the D36 hello proves key K, the room requires a grant for
  K that verifies against those issuers (full `verifyGrant`, not `readGrantOrigin`) and serves K
  only the partitions that grant covers: catch-up and live fan-out are narrowed by **entitlement ∩
  interest**, with a scoped coverage whose scope names the entitlement (D23, and D24's warning
  that it must be the entitlement, not the interest). Peer to peer, a sender in a granted mesh
  never sends an unsealed partition's event to a peer whose verified grant does not cover it. The
  relay operator still reads unsealed payloads.
- **C · B, plus sealed partitions where the operator must not read.** Everything in B; the kinds
  that must hold against the relay and against carriers (Company OS: DMs, HR) are declared
  `sealed: true`, and the missing issuer half is built: mint a partition key, wrap it into issued
  grants, rotate the epoch when a member is removed. The relay, a reverse proxy, and any device
  carrying the bytes see ciphertext for those kinds.
- **D · Seal everything.** Every partition sealed, the relay trusted for nothing but availability.
  Rejected for a company app: the authority (search, projections, AI walking the graph, CDC into
  Postgres) reads content by design, and RFC-0024 O5 already chose "no E2E for v1" for the same
  reason.

**Recommendation: C.** B is the default for every hosted room; sealing is a per-kind choice the
app makes for the data its operator must not read. RFC-0017 says both sentences, and says _"a
room name is a bearer secret"_ for exactly the one case where it is still true — a relay started
with `open: true` (§3 below).

## 2 · Rooms and tickets

**Mapping.**

- **(i) One room per partition instance** (RFC-0024 §4.1). Access _is_ the room, so the relay needs
  no per-event narrowing. Costs a socket per partition per device: a Company OS member in 30
  projects and 200 DMs holds 230 sockets.
- **(ii) One room per tenant, narrowed per socket by grant.** One socket per device per workspace;
  the relay narrows each socket by its grant, using the interest machinery that already exists.

**Recommendation: (ii)**, with (i) still expressible (a room whose only partition is one instance
is the degenerate case of (ii), and needs nothing extra).

**Ticket.**

- **(a) The grant is the ticket, checked in band after the hello.** Bound to the proven key by
  construction (the grant names a device; the hello proved one). No new artifact, no header (a
  browser cannot set headers on a WebSocket), no bearer token in a URL. The catch-up is held until
  a qualifying grant arrives, or refused as **`ungranted`** — a new refusal code, so a new frozen
  vector in `relay-vectors.json`. Non-fatal: the client renews and retries.
- **(b) A separate signed room ticket at HTTP upgrade**, checked in `verifyJoin`. A new signed
  artifact with its own vectors; carried in a query string or subprotocol because of the browser;
  a bearer token until bound to the hello's key, which only happens after the upgrade anyway.
- **(c) Both.** (a) is the mesh's check; `verifyJoin` stays the app's own HTTP gate (a cookie, a
  rate limit).

**Recommendation: (a), with `verifyJoin` kept as (c)'s HTTP gate.** A room ticket minted from a
grant is the grant; a second artifact would be a second answer to "which device may see which
partition" (the same argument grant key 8 makes for keys).

## 3 · Revocation and defaults

- **Socket.** A newer grant for K arriving at the room (newest-issued wins, as `GrantCache` already
  keys it) that narrows K's entitlement **closes K's socket** with `revoked`, non-fatal; the client
  re-joins and is served the narrower scope. Closing rather than re-narrowing in place keeps one
  code path for "what may this socket see" — the join. A grant that lapses (past `expiresAt` plus
  the room's grace) closes the socket the same way.
- **Peer to peer.** A sender re-reads the far side's grant on every send; a narrowed grant stops the
  next event, and a link whose peer lost every shared partition is closed.
- **Keys (sealed kinds).** Removing a member rotates every sealed partition they held: the issuer
  mints epoch n+1 and re-issues grants with it to the remaining members. The removed device keeps
  what it already read (no scheme undoes a read) and reads nothing written after.
- **Defaults.** The hosted presets — `adapters/relay-node` and `adapters/cloudflare-do` — refuse to
  start (a definition error, thrown) without either `trust` or an explicit `open: true`, and default
  `announce` to `false`. `@syncmesh/relay`'s Bun `startRelay` (what every test and local dev
  mounts) keeps today's open default.
  An `open: true` relay logs, once, the sentence _"a room name is a bearer secret"_.

## 4 · `fields: { column: rule }`

An event carries every column it writes, signed. A column cannot be removed from an event without
breaking its signature, so a field rule cannot hold on any path that forwards events — relay or
peer. E06's _"evaluated by the relay before rows leave it"_ is not possible either: the relay reads
no payloads.

- **(a) Enforced only where an authority serves rows rather than events** — a snapshot answer the
  authority builds, and E27's row path when it lands — via one pure `readable(schema, grant, table,
  row)` that drops the columns the grant fails. On a device it is cosmetic (`can`-style), and the
  docs say so. A table whose restricted column must be secret from members is modelled as a
  separate table in a narrower (or sealed) partition — the rule that _does_ hold on every path.
- **(b) Per-column sealing** — restricted columns encrypted under a separate key. A new wire shape
  and a second key system; not justified by any consumer yet.
- **(c) Drop the task**, and say "model it as a partition".

**Recommendation: (a).**

## What this does not decide

- Write admission at the relay (RFC-0024 R1–R4) and the D32 report frame — the relay still stores
  and fans out writes honest receivers will quarantine.
- Authority visibility (D24) — a sibling builds it; the entitlement-scoped cursor here is the same
  shape and the two must share it, not fork it.
- Metadata: presence, cursors frames and the grant cache still reach every socket in the room.

## Decision

Decided by the owner in the gap/read-permissions thread, 2026-11-07, taking every recommendation:

1. **Posture C.** Rooms are grant-gated: the relay holds public trust config (issuer keys), verifies
   the joiner's grant after the D36 hello, and serves each socket only the partitions its grant
   covers — catch-up and live fan-out alike, scoped coverage naming the entitlement. Peer to peer,
   a sender in a granted mesh never sends a peer an event of a partition the peer's verified grant
   does not cover. Kinds the operator must not read are `sealed: true`, and the issuer half is
   built: mint a partition key, wrap it into issued grants, rotate the epoch on removal.
2. **One room per tenant, the grant is the ticket** (ii)(a). Checked in band after the hello; a
   joiner with no qualifying grant is refused as `ungranted` (new code, new frozen vector). The
   grant binds to a proven key only: a gated room refuses a bare v1 join, which an open room may
   still take on trust, as `unproven`. `verifyJoin` stays the app's own HTTP gate.
3. **A narrowing or lapsed grant closes the socket** with `revoked`, non-fatal; the device
   re-joins under the narrower scope. The hosted presets (`adapters/relay-node`,
   `adapters/cloudflare-do`) refuse to start without `trust` or an explicit `open: true`, and
   default `announce` to `false`; `@syncmesh/relay`'s Bun `startRelay` stays open.
4. **`fields` holds only where an authority serves rows** (a); on a device it is cosmetic, and a
   column that must be secret from members belongs in its own table in a narrower or sealed
   partition.

## What building it settled (2026-11-07)

Consequences the build met, recorded so nobody rediscovers them:

- **Onboarding by grant-request does not cross a gated room.** A device with no grant has no
  seat, so flow A's `grant-request` cannot reach the issuer through it. Grants are handed out of
  band — RFC-0024's `POST /syncmesh/grant` — and a device registers its own before it dials.
- **The ticket is waited for, briefly.** A device sends its grants straight after its join; the
  room holds the join for `trust.wait` (default 5 s) and seats it the moment a qualifying grant
  arrives. A gated room caches only grants that verify, so a forged core stamped later cannot
  evict a device's real grant — a revocation anyone could otherwise send.
- **A hole the gate leaves is told, not left.** Live fan-out that passes an event over sends an
  empty last page with a scoped coverage up to it (relay), or a `withheld` frame naming the
  sequences (peer links — named, never a reach, because a radio drops frames and a reach would
  carry a receiver past an event lost in transit). Without either, the author's later events
  sat in the receiver's holdback. Both are frozen in `relay-vectors.json` / `frame-vectors.json`.
- **The device accepts the entitlement scope.** It joins with its own interest and adopts a
  coverage scoped either to that or to that cut by its own grant, reading which before the page's
  grants register (one of them can be its own, re-joining it); it re-joins when its own grant
  changes what it may read.
- **Peers: unsealed content does not cross a device that may not read it.** That is the rule as
  decided, and it has a price — a chain whose middle may not read a partition does not carry it.
  Sealing is how content crosses a carrier; sealed partitions are never withheld.
- **A link with no handshake is carried whole.** Only `linkTransport` attaches without a proven
  peer; every radio handshakes. The gate judges the peer the handshake proved, never a name a
  frame gives.
- **A snapshot answer is gated too**, since it is rows in the clear: served under what the asker
  may read.
- **Unpinned events reach every seated device**, per D24's settlement of its problem 3.
