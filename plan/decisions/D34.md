id: D34
title: A stamp may not run ahead of the receiver's clock by more than the drift bound
status: decided
decided: B
epics: [E01, E02, E09, E24]

## Context

Every `lww` cell is won by the higher stamp, and a stamp is `(hlc, peer)` with the HLC's physical
half read from the author's wall clock (`kernel/src/hlc.ts`). `createHlcClock` has had a
`maxDrift` clamp on `receive` since RFC-0003, but nothing set it: `client/src/boot.ts` built the
clock as `createHlcClock({ now })`, and no rung of the ladder looked at a stamp at all. So a
device with its clock a year fast — by accident or on purpose — won every cell it touched on every
peer, and every peer's clock ratcheted a year forward on receipt, after which every honest write
from every honest device lost to it too. The audit (2026-09-26, gap №1) called this the one that
corrupts data rather than merely leaking it.

## Options

- **A · Clamp only.** Pass `maxDrift` to the clock. Stops the receiver's clock being dragged, but
  the event is still folded with its own stamp and still wins the cells. Half a fix.
- **B · Clamp, and a rung.** The same bound in two seams: the clock clamps a remote stamp to
  `now + maxDrift`, and the validator's first rung refuses an event stamped beyond it as
  `ClockAhead`. Refused means parked (D13): the author's cursor stops below it, and a retry admits
  it once the wall clock has caught up with the claim — a fast clock delays that device's writes by
  its own error and nothing else. Ahead only: the past is history, and history is exactly what a
  device offline for a month has to be able to fold.
- **C · Reject at the fold with a clamped stamp.** Fold the event but with its stamp clamped to
  the bound. Rejected: two peers with different clocks would fold the same event under different
  stamps, which is divergence by construction (book ch. 2).

## Decision

**B.** `DEFAULT_MAX_DRIFT` is five minutes (`kernel/src/hlc.ts`) — wide enough for a phone that has
never met an NTP server, narrow enough that a clock set to next year cannot win a cell.
`MeshOptions.clockDrift` overrides it, set identically on every peer like every other rung of the
ladder. `ValidatorOptions.maxDrift` arms the rung; absent it, or absent `now`, no stamp is judged,
so every validator built before the bound existed keeps its verdicts. The verdict carries the
stamp and the limit as ISO strings so a person can see which clock is wrong.

## What this costs

- One instant comparison per received event.
- A device whose clock is more than five minutes fast has its writes parked on every peer until
  the peers' clocks reach the stamp. That is the correct failure: the alternative was that device
  silently overwriting everyone.

## Watch out

- **Parked, not lost — but parked stalls the stream.** The author's cursor stops below the parked
  event, so its later events wait behind it (gap audit №16). The retry that clears it is the
  ordinary quarantine retry; nothing schedules one on a timer yet. A device that fixes its clock
  and reconnects is re-offered and admitted; one that never reconnects stays parked, which is the
  same as any other refusal.
- **The rung reads `now`.** Chapter 6's NEVER — no environment inputs in policy — is about the
  policy AST, which stays pure. This rung is admission, beside the grace window that already reads
  a clock (RFC-0016), and it is one-directional so two peers can only disagree about *when* an
  event folds, never about *what* the fold produces once it does.
- **Do not lower the default to chase a test.** Five minutes is a claim about phones. A test that
  needs a tighter bound passes `clockDrift`.
