## Context

The exploration froze canonical CBOR events (7 integer keys, sorted maps, absent optionals skipped) signed with detached Ed25519 in a `[core, sig]` envelope, and shipped `research/wire-vectors.json`. A Rust/Swift port is 'compatible' when it reproduces those bytes. You can inherit that wire or design a new one — but not change it later without invalidating every log.

## Options

- **A · Keep the wire and the vectors** — you get an exact oracle for E03 on day one; the security model (signature over received bytes → relays forward byte-identically holding no keys) is proven.
- **B · New wire, same principles** — e.g. different key layout, protobuf, or a version-negotiated envelope from the start. Regenerate vectors. Costs the oracle; gains whatever you change.
- **C · Keep the event core, redesign the transport frames** (join/catch-up/ack) — those were JSON-with-base64 and never frozen; they are the weaker half.

## What the research points to

A for the event core and grant envelope — they are the part that has to be right forever and they are tested. C is free on top: the relay frames are not frozen and version negotiation belongs there (D14). B only if you find a concrete flaw in the core; the exploration did not.
**Prior art — automerge.** Its sync uses Bloom filters (10 bits/entry, 7 probes, ~1 % FPR, parameters carried in the wire) because a DAG of content-addressed changes has no compact exact summary. Ours has per-author monotonic sequence numbers, so a cursor map is exact in O(peers) bytes — keep cursors. Take the `in_flight` flag (never send a second batch to a peer until the first is acknowledged) and the pure-function shape of the per-peer step. See `research/prior-art-automerge.md` §5.

## Watch out

The signature covers the *received* bytes. Anything that rewrites bytes — an upcaster at decode time, a 'helpful' normaliser — breaks every signature. That constraint decides D13 too.
