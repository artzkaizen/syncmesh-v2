## Context

Cursors, typing indicators, 'who is here' — 60 Hz signals that must never enter the log, the snapshot or the compaction floor, and must never build a backlog on a stalled socket.

## Options

- **A · A separate ephemeral tier** — its own wire kind, conflated at all three hops (sender, relay, receiver), TTL *and* explicit clear, keyed `(account, peerId)` so two tabs group under one person, signed so the relay still cannot forge.
- **B · Not in the engine** — apps do presence over their own WebSocket.

## What the research points to

A, after the core is stable; it is fully designed in RFC-0020 §6 and it is table stakes for every collaborative demo. The market-data lesson is the design: conflate, never queue.
**Prior art — automerge-repo.** Ephemeral messages carry `(sessionId, count)` so a gossiping peer can drop what it has already seen (a 1000-entry seen ring); `Presence` on top does broadcast-on-change, heartbeat when idle, TTL pruning, and a hello on join. Validates the design here line for line; take the loop-breaker. See `research/prior-art-automerge.md` §7.

## Watch out

Keying presence by connection instead of account is reflectdb's documented wart.

## Decision

**A — a separate ephemeral tier, signed, conflated at every hop, never in the log.**

Presence is its own wire kind with its own core (`v, peerId, topic, partition, session,
count, value, expires`), signed by the device exactly as an event is — so a relay still
cannot forge a cursor — and that is where the resemblance ends. It is never appended, never
folded, never snapshotted, never counted by a cursor, never carried in catch-up or
anti-entropy. `grep` for the log and you will not find it: the engine does not know the tier
exists.

The three hops each keep only the last value per `(topic, partition, peer)`. That is the
market-data lesson and the reason a stalled socket gets the current cursor rather than a
backlog of stale ones: conflate, never queue. A frame carries `(session, count)` — session
random per process start, count monotonic — and every hop keeps the highest count it has
seen per session, so a frame that comes back around a gossip loop is dropped on arrival
rather than forwarded forever.

Identity is `(account, peerId)`, never the connection: two tabs of one person are two peers
under one account, and a reconnect does not become a second ghost. That is reflectdb's
documented wart, avoided by construction. Expiry is `ttlMs` from the manifest **and** an
explicit `clear()`, because a tab that closes gracefully should vanish now, not in five
seconds, and a tab that crashes should vanish eventually without anyone's help.

`set()` returns `void`, deliberately, against this codebase's rule that failures are values:
the call sits in a pointer-move handler at 60 Hz, and a `Result` nobody unwraps trains people
to ignore return values everywhere else. Misuse — an unknown topic, a value failing its
shape, an instance of the wrong kind — is a `panic` at the definition boundary or a dropped
frame with a diagnostic, never a silent lie and never an exception into a pointer handler.

B (apps do presence over their own socket) is rejected for the reason RFC-0020 gives: it is
table stakes for the collaborative apps this engine targets, and leaving it out means every
app rebuilds signing, TTL, conflation and identity — badly.
