## Context

> **Naming superseded by D25 (2026-08-29).** The strategy `lww` is now spelled `lastWrite`, and
> the deferred option C below — cell-level CRDTs — was built as `counter` and `set` and then
> deleted. What A decided is unchanged: field-level merge by HLC stamp, with a declarable
> strategy per column. Only the names moved.

## Context

Concurrent edits from offline devices must converge on every peer with no coordinator. The kernel is the one piece a wrong choice cannot be patched around.

## Options

- **A · Field-level last-writer-wins by HLC stamp, tombstones, plus declarative per-column strategies (`lww` | `max` | `min`) — all lattice joins** (commutative, associative, idempotent). `counter`/`set` later, needing a new change kind.
- **B · Row-level LWW** — simpler; two people editing different fields of one row lose one edit. reflectdb offers this as `lww`; their own docs recommend against it for collaborative data.
- **C · Full CRDTs per column** (text CRDTs, OR-sets, counters) — the most capable; large wire and kernel cost; text CRDTs are a project of their own.
- **D · Custom resolver functions** (reflectdb's `conflict: custom`) — rejected in the exploration: a merge function runs on every device and must be pure; the dangerous class of impurity (stable here, different elsewhere) is undetectable locally and fails silently.

## What the research points to

A. Field-level LWW is what makes 'title on phone A, body on phone B' work with zero configuration; `max`/`min` cover bids and high-water marks without touching the wire. Model the rest away — append-only child rows instead of one mutable shared field. Revisit C only for collaborative prose, as a separate column kind.
## Watch out

insert × insert on the same key: the RFC's matrix said whole-row replace, the kernel field-merged. Pick one *before* a second implementation exists; the exploration blessed field-merge. `compareStamp` off by one `<=` silently breaks dedup under equal HLCs.

## Decision

**A — field-level last-writer-wins by HLC stamp, tombstones for deletes, plus declarative per-column strategies (`lww` | `max` | `min`). Every strategy is a lattice join.**

- Two devices editing different fields of the same row both keep their edit with zero configuration; that is the whole reason for field-level over row-level (B).
- `max` / `min` cover the cases people reach for custom merges (bids, high-water marks) without touching the wire; the stamp breaks exact ties.
- Anything that needs more than LWW is modelled as append-only child rows, not a mutable shared field.
- A is itself a CRDT: an LWW-register per cell (the same base Automerge, Ditto and Electric use). C is the richer family — sequence CRDTs for text, OR-Sets, PN-Counters — which merge *inside* a cell. C is not rejected, it is scheduled: **E26** adds `counter`, `set` and `text` column kinds as additional strategies + change kinds on top of A, once the wire (E03) and schema (E05) exist. Nothing built under A is undone by it.
- D (custom resolvers) stays rejected: a merge function that is pure on one device and not on another fails silently, and nothing local can detect it.
